Frequently Asked Questions

Do towel warmers use a lot of electricity?

No. Most towel warmers for home draw between 100 and 300 watts, closer to a light bulb than a space heater. A bar rack like the sawlece freestanding model run for an hour a day costs a few dollars a month at average US rates. The cabinet and bucket models heat in bursts rather than continuously, so they cost even less.

What is the difference between a bucket towel warmer and a heated towel rack?

A bucket or cabinet warmer, like the StateRiver 23L or VIPBATH 20L, surrounds the towel with heat inside an enclosed space, so it warms fast and can dry a damp towel between uses. A heated towel rack, like the sawlece freestanding model, hangs the towel on warm bars and is better at keeping it dry over a full day. Pick a bucket for fast warmth, a rack for ongoing drying.

Are towel warmers safe to leave on?

The safest towel warmers for home include an automatic shut-off timer, which every pick in this guide offers in some form. The VERYTOP and StateRiver cut power after their heating cycle ends, so you are not relying on memory. We still suggest running any unit on a timer or smart plug if you leave the house in a hurry, and keeping it away from the tub edge where it could get splashed.

Can a towel warmer dry a wet towel, or just warm a dry one?

Enclosed bucket and cabinet warmers like the StateRiver 23L can dry a damp towel because they trap heat around it. Open bar racks warm faster than they dry, so a soaking towel comes out warm but still moist. If drying matters as much as warmth, choose an enclosed model.
