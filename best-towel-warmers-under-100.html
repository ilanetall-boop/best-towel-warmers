You step out of a hot shower onto a cold floor, reach for a cold, damp towel, and accept it as one of those small daily annoyances. Frequently Asked Questions

Can you really get a good towel warmer under $100?

Yes. Bucket-style and compact wall-mounted models sell under $100, including the INNOKA 2-in-1 at $75.00, the DR.PREPARE 23L at $82.48, the Keenray at $94.99, and the APORDROUCA wall-mounted unit at $98.99. These warm a folded towel in minutes and cost far less than the large freestanding and multi-bar racks, which tend to run well above $100.

What's the difference between a bucket warmer and a bar warmer?

A bucket warmer, like the DR.PREPARE 23L or the Keenray 21L, is an enclosed cylinder you roll a towel into; it heats the whole towel at once and warms fastest. A bar warmer, whether freestanding like the Sawlece 5-Bar or wall-mounted like the DAWEYEAL 9-Bar, drapes towels over heated rails, which doubles as a drying rack and a permanent fixture but takes longer to get a towel hot.

Are towel warmers expensive to run?

No. Most towel warmers draw about the same power as a few light bulbs, and many of the models here include timers that shut the unit off after a set period. Running one for an hour a day costs only pennies, and the auto-shutoff timers keep it from staying on all day if you forget.

Should I get freestanding or wall-mounted?

Choose freestanding if you rent or don't want to drill: the Sawlece 5-Bar plugs in, stands on the floor, and moves with you. Choose wall-mounted, like the APORDROUCA ($98.99) or the 9-Bar DAWEYEAL ($228.88), if you own your home and want to free up floor space with a permanent fixture.

Which towel warmer heats a towel the fastest?

Bucket-style warmers are the quickest because they surround the rolled towel with heat on all sides. The DR.PREPARE 23L, Keenray 21L, and INNOKA 2-in-1 all warm a folded towel through in minutes, faster than draping one over the bars of a rack-style unit.
