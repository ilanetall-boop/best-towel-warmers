Frequently Asked Questions

Do the best with timer heated towel racks use a lot of electricity?

Most timer-equipped heated towel racks draw between 60 and 150 watts, similar to a couple of light bulbs. The timer is what keeps the cost down, because it shuts the rack off after a set window instead of letting it run all day. Run one for two hours each morning and you are looking at a few dollars a month on a typical US electricity rate.

How long does a heated towel rack take to warm a towel?

A damp towel hung on a warm rack feels noticeably drier and cozier in about 30 to 45 minutes, and fully dry in roughly two to three hours depending on towel thickness and bathroom humidity. The timer helps here too, because you can set the rack to switch on before your shower so the bars are already hot when you step out.

Can you install a with timer heated towel rack yourself?

Yes, most of the wall-mounted timer racks we compared are plug-in models, so you mount the bracket, hang the unit, and plug it into a nearby GFCI outlet. No electrician is needed for those. Hardwired models that tie into your wall wiring are a different story, and you should hire a licensed electrician for any rack that connects directly to a junction box in a wet room.

How many bars do I need on a heated towel rack?

A 6-bar rack like our budget R FLORY pick handles one bath towel plus a hand towel, which suits a single user or a small bathroom. If two people share the space or you use oversized bath sheets, step up to a 9 or 10-bar rack such as the DAWEYEAL or our top-pick BLARALA so both towels can warm at once.

Is it safe to leave a heated towel rack on?

The timer makes this a non-issue, which is the main reason we only recommend racks that have one. You program a daily on-and-off window and the rack powers down on its own, so you are not relying on memory to switch it off. The stainless steel models here run warm to the touch rather than scalding, but as with any electrical appliance in a bathroom, plug it into a GFCI outlet.
